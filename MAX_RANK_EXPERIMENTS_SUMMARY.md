# X-Ray Conformal Calibration Experiments: Permanent Archive Summary (Experiments 001–031)

> This document is the permanent, dense record of everything learned across `experiments/001` through `experiments/031` (the max-rank / structured-BCOPS line of work), preserved after the raw experiment folders were deleted to reduce repo/context size ahead of the pivot to the statement-level, text+image pipeline. A raw backup tarball of the deleted folders exists outside this git repo (see bottom of this file for location) in case a specific number or plot needs to be recovered.

## 1. Baseline Numbers

**The "91.9% / 89.7% coverage, 81.2% decisive accuracy, 45.7% abstention" figures are NOT from any experiment in this repo.** They are cited repeatedly (Exp001, 002, 003) as `reference_baseline_table1` — explicitly labeled as the **MOST proposal's Table 1 / CLAUDE.md reference number**, from a **5-pathology CheXpert study on a different, larger dataset**, shown only for context, never as a reproduction target. Exact quoted values: positive coverage 91.9%, negative coverage 89.7%, decisive accuracy 81.2%, Unc.→Abs. rate 45.7%.

The actual **independent per-label BCOPS baseline for this pipeline's own 13-label MIMIC-CXR model** was computed progressively more rigorously across Exp001→003→004→018→021:

| Experiment | Data | n_calib/n_test | Mean pos. cov | Mean neg. cov | Mean decisive acc. | Mean abstention |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exp001 (proxy 50/50 split of one test CSV) | leaky/proxy | 1790/1790 | 93.0% | 90.1% | 75.1% | 51.1% |
| Exp003/004 (real conformal split, first non-proxy baseline) | leaky (32.8% subject overlap, discovered later) | 2741/3580 | 89.48% | 88.92% | **79.46%** | 45.58% |
| Exp018 (refit on subject-clean data) | clean | 8118/11761 | 90.9% | 89.8% | **79.5%** (~79.48% in Exp021) | 46.4% |

So the pipeline's own honest independent-BCOPS baseline is **≈79.46–79.5% mean decisive accuracy**, essentially unchanged whether measured on the leaky split or the later de-leaked split — this is the number every subsequent "improvement" is measured against, not the MOST-proposal's 81.2%.

Hierarchy-violation rate for the baseline: 2.40% on Exp001's proxy split; **1.65% (59/3580)** on the real leaky split (Exp003/004); **1.86% (219/11761)** on the clean split (Exp021).

## 2. Chronological Narrative

### Phase A: Naive joint calibration attempts (Exp001–004)
- **Exp001 (max-cluster BCOPS):** First attempt at structured calibration — connected-component clustering (hard hierarchy edges ∪ PMI soft edges) with naive **max-nonconformity pooling** across the whole cluster. Result: hierarchy violations eliminated (2.40%→0.00%) but by driving 88–99.5% abstention on the 6-member Lung Opacity cluster; 3 of 6 labels fell to sub-chance decisive accuracy (Consolidation 33.3%, Lung Lesion 29.4%, Pneumonia 57.1%). Mean decisive accuracy regressed 75.1%→69.2% (**ESCALATED**). Diagnosis: full connected-component clustering is too coarse a calibration unit for max-pooling — it transitively fused 6 labels through the Lung Opacity hub.
- **Exp002 (pairwise-max):** Fix — treat each edge as its own 2-member group, combine per-label thresholds via max(presence)/min(absence) across all of a label's edges. Recovered most of the lost accuracy (75.1%→74.5%, vs. Exp001's 69.2%) while keeping 0% hierarchy violations. Still on the proxy split.
- **Exp003 (pairwise-max on the real conformal split):** Moved to the pipeline's real calibration file (`conformal_scores.csv`, 2741 rows) and fixed a coverage-validity gap (calibration set must be the target label's own population, not the pooled a-OR-b population). Coverage validity now held cleanly, but **Fracture collapsed to 16.8% decisive accuracy (below chance)** — traced to small-sample noise (only 69 positive calibration rows) combined with an uninformative max-partner (Pneumothorax). **ESCALATED**.
- **Exp004 (dominance-fraction gate):** Introduced the concept of a **"gate"** for the first time: a rule that decides, per directed candidate edge, whether a partner label's probability is *actually informative* (not just co-occurring in ground truth) before trusting it in a max-pooled score. Rule: drop a candidate if the partner's term dominates the max on >50% of the target's own rows (plus an n≥50 backstop). Result: **Fracture recovered to 77.3%** (above its own 69.5% baseline); **13-label mean decisive accuracy 79.70%**, essentially matching the independent baseline (79.46%) while still improving hierarchy violations (1.12% vs. 1.65%). This is the first **closed, stable structured-calibration result** — "gated-pairwise-max" — and became the standing fallback method used everywhere downstream.

### Phase B: Max-Rank (Exp005–011) — the core structured-conformal method
- **Exp005:** Implemented Timans et al. 2025 "Max-Rank" (AISTATS/arXiv:2311.10900), a genuinely **joint/family-wise** rank-based multiple-testing correction (FWER control), ported literally from the paper's own reference code (Listing 1), applied to the same 6-member Lung Opacity cluster. Result: **mean decisive accuracy 82.52%** on those 6 labels, beating both the independent baseline (78.60%) and gated-pairwise (77.65%) restricted to the same 6 labels. 5 of 6 labels improved (some by 10+pp); **Lung Lesion regressed to 55.4%** (still above chance, unlike Exp001's collapse) — traced to "population dilution": Lung Lesion is only 6.3% of its family's shared calibration population, so the shared rank threshold gets set by rows that aren't Lung-Lesion-positive.
- **Exp006:** Tested Max-Rank on two clean 2-member clusters to distinguish "is population dilution hierarchy-specific or size-specific?" Result: the hierarchy-linked pair (Cardiomegaly/Enlarged Cardiomediastinum, prevalence ratio 1.10) showed **zero regression** — both members improved. The PMI-only pair (Fracture/Pneumothorax, prevalence ratio 2.03) **regressed** (Fracture 69.5%→62.15%). Conclusion: the mechanism is **prevalence-ratio/population-dilution driven**, not hierarchy-hub-specific, and recurs even at the smallest possible family size (m=2).

### Phase C: The gate-design search (Exp007–011) — "what is a gate," why introduced
A **"gate"** in this project means a pre-calibration-time rule that decides *which labels are safe to include in a joint Max-Rank family* vs. which should fall back to the already-proven gated-pairwise method — introduced because Max-Rank's raw FWER guarantee is provably valid but can be uninformatively wide for minority/diluted members (Exp005/006's Lung Lesion/Fracture findings). Five successive gate designs were tried, each rejected on principled or empirical grounds:
- **Exp007 (prevalence-gated Max-Rank):** first working gate — own true-positive/negative count ≥100 (derived from only 10 evidence points across 3 clusters). Full 13-label hybrid: **mean decisive accuracy 83.83%**, beating gated-pairwise (79.70%) by +4.13pp; both known weak spots (Lung Lesion, Fracture) correctly routed to fallback and recovered exactly to gated-pairwise's numbers (80.9%, 77.3%).
- **Exp008 (gate validation, Task A/B):** Found the "≥100" count gate is *stable* under calibration-set bootstrap (only Fracture flips, 0.2%) but has **no principled derivation** — it doesn't match Timans' own `n≥100m` finite-sample rule (which is satisfied by 177x margin for the 6-member cluster even though Lung Lesion still fails), and the same own-count (67/69) behaves fine under independent calibration but fails under Max-Rank — proving the mechanism is Max-Rank-specific (rank-position mismatch), not generic small-sample noise. **ESCALATED** (unprincipled threshold).
- **Exp009 (abstention-ratio gate):** Tom's replacement proposal — gate on how much a member's own-class abstention balloons under joint vs. independent calibration. **Failed entirely**: none of 5 pre-registered tolerances (nor any of 6 alternative constructions) recovered the known 8-PASS/2-FAIL split; Edema (a known-good member) had the *highest* abstention-ratio of all 10 points. **ESCALATED and rejected.**
- **Exp010 (cross-fitted decisive-accuracy-delta gate):** The mechanistically correct statistic — repeated fit/held splits of the calibration set, measuring each candidate's held-out decisive-accuracy delta (joint − independent). Cleanly separated the known split (10/10 for 3 of 4 tolerances, 3.45pp gap). But **bootstrap validation disqualified it**: Fracture flip rate 32.6%, Pneumothorax 26.6% — both far over the 5% stability bar, both from the smallest cluster (Cluster B, n=198). **ESCALATED**, closing recommendation: fallback to Exp004's gated-pairwise (79.70%) as the headline method.
- **Exp011 (z-margin gate):** Final iteration — a z-score (distance in standard errors from the −2pp decision boundary) instead of the raw point estimate. Improved stability substantially (Fracture flip rate 32.6%→1.5%) but **Pneumothorax still flipped 16.2%** of the time (>5% bar). **ESCALATED, final rejection of the gate-design line.**

**Bottom line of the gate-design search:** across 5 distinct calibration-time statistics (own-count, abstention-ratio, raw cross-fit delta, z-margin, and later Exp030's re-test on a different pair), the mechanism (population dilution distorting Max-Rank's shared-rank threshold for minority members) was always correctly diagnosed, but **no gate statistic was ever stably estimable for the smallest family (Cluster B, {Fracture, Pneumothorax}, n=198)** — its true joint-vs-independent effect sits too close to any reasonable decision boundary for n=198 to resolve reliably.

### Phase D: Locking the final hybrid (Exp012–016)
- **Exp012 (final hybrid headline):** Pure aggregation, no new fitting. Locked routing: 7 members (5-member reduced Lung Opacity family + 2-member Cluster A) on Max-Rank; 6 members (Lung Lesion, Fracture, Pneumothorax [excluded by z-margin gate] + Pleural Effusion, Pleural Other, Support Devices [zero-edge]) on gated-pairwise fallback. **Headline: mean decisive accuracy 84.00%** (+4.55pp vs. independent 79.46%, +4.30pp vs. gated-pairwise 79.70%), both 95% bootstrap CIs excluding zero ([+4.06,+5.03]pp and [+3.87,+4.76]pp respectively). Hierarchy violation rate: 1.648%→0.475% (final hybrid). Mean marginal coverage 91.90%/92.97% (pos/neg). No label regressed below its own independent baseline.
- **Exp013:** Added a bootstrap CI to the violation-rate claim: independent 1.648% [1.229%,2.067%] vs. hybrid 0.475% [0.251%,0.726%]; the two CIs don't overlap; the reduction's CI [0.810pp, 1.537pp] excludes 0.
- **Exp014:** Confirmed the decisive-rate tradeoff shape matches Tom's predicted pattern (large decisive-rate drops on the 5-member Lung Opacity group, e.g. Edema 68.8%→17.2%; near-flat on Cluster A, -2.0/-2.4pp).
- **Exp015:** Filled a gap — computed *independent-calibration joint coverage* for the first time (previously only Max-Rank's joint coverage had been checked). Found the motivating problem statement directly: independent per-label calibration gives the 6-member cluster only **38.19% held-out joint presence coverage** against a 90% target (vs. Max-Rank's 90.45%); the 5-member deployed family only 67.18% (vs. Max-Rank's 93.58%); Cluster A (2 members, already fairly balanced) only mildly short (89.36% vs 90%).
- **Exp016:** Found an unrelated, un-fixed problem: "No Finding" is a deterministic-exclusion label (by dataset construction, No Finding=1 zeroes all other pathologies) never touched by any hierarchy/Max-Rank work. ~25% of test rows are decisively No-Finding-positive, and 16–19% of those also carry a decisively-positive pathology at the same time — a real logical contradiction, ~8–10x larger than the residual hierarchy-violation rate, incidentally (not by design) reduced 4.665%→3.855% by the hybrid as a side effect.

### Phase E: The "clean data" rederivation (Exp017–021) — data-quality fix
- **Exp017 discovered a subject-level leakage problem**: the newer, larger calibration file (9,229 rows, 6,797 subjects) and test file (11,938 rows, 8,027 subjects) shared **2,633 of 8,027 test subjects (32.8%)** — i.e., the same patients appeared in both calibration and test, violating exchangeability/i.i.d. assumptions underlying every coverage guarantee in Exp001–016. Fix: re-bucketed at the **patient (subject_id) level**, not row level, using the same Mondrian stratification logic as the main pipeline, seed 42, target ~44/56 split. Result: **new-calibration 8,118 rows / 4,952 subjects (post uncertain-label filter), new-test 11,761 rows / 6,827 subjects, 0 subject overlap** (mechanically asserted). Pleural Other flagged as still LOW-n (72 positives) but not worse than before (69 previously).
- **Exp018** refit independent BCOPS + pairwise-max + gated-pairwise fresh on the clean split. All three methods landed within ~0.5pp of their leaky-split numbers (gated-pairwise: 79.70%→80.11%, +0.4pp) — reassuring mechanical stability. One PMI soft edge changed: Fracture–Pneumothorax (PMI 0.773, no longer qualifies) was replaced by Fracture–Pleural Other (PMI 1.231, border case).
- **Exp019** re-ran the entire z-margin gate/cross-fit procedure fresh on the clean calibration data. **Result: REPLICATED exactly** — identical 7-included/3-excluded partition, and Pneumothorax's bootstrap flip rate *dropped* from the disqualifying 16.2% (leaky) to a comfortable 0.7% (clean) — i.e. the previously-marginal, leakage-affected instability resolved once the leakage was removed, though it had already been excluded by the gate regardless.
- **Exp020** refit Max-Rank on the clean data for the confirmed 5-member family + Cluster A. Joint coverage held-out: 5-member family 91.09% (presence)/89.16% (absence), vs. independent's 59.38%/64.42%; Cluster A 89.27%/89.48% vs. independent's 87.53%/88.58%. **Important finding: the independent-calibration collapse got *worse*, not better, after removing leakage** (67.18%→59.38% for the 5-member family), meaning the original leaky numbers had actually *understated* how badly independent per-label calibration fails on correlated families — strengthening rather than weakening the paper's motivating claim. A refreshed 6-member (incl. Lung Lesion) independent-collapse number replicated closely: 38.19% (leaky) → 39.75% (clean).
- **Exp021 (final hybrid rerun on clean data)** replayed Exp012's exact aggregation logic on the clean split. **Result: replicated and slightly stronger.** Mean decisive accuracy: independent baseline 79.48%, final hybrid **84.81%**, delta **+5.34pp** (95% CI [+5.09,+5.57]pp, vs. leaky's +4.55pp). Hierarchy violation: independent 1.8621% (219/11761) → hybrid 0.5187% (61/11761), a **72.15% relative reduction** (vs. leaky's 71.19%). Both of Tom's pre-registered pass/fail bands (delta≥+2.0pp with CI excluding 0; relative violation reduction ≥40%) were met, with margin.

## 3. Final Verdict — Best/Final Max-Rank-Based Result

**The headline, final, most rigorously validated result is Experiment 021 (final hybrid rerun on subject-clean, leakage-free data):**

| Metric | Independent baseline (clean) | Final hybrid (clean) | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mean 13-label decisive accuracy | 79.48% | **84.81%** | **+5.34pp** (95% CI [+5.09, +5.57]pp) |
| Hierarchy violation rate | 1.8621% (219/11,761) | **0.5187%** (61/11,761) | **-72.15% relative** |
| n_test | 11,761 (0 subject overlap with calibration) | | |
| Mean marginal coverage (pos/neg) | ~90% target by construction | ≥90% (family-joint for the 7 Max-Rank members) | |

Routing: **7 labels on Max-Rank** (5-member reduced Lung Opacity family: Atelectasis, Consolidation, Edema, Lung Opacity, Pneumonia — Lung Lesion excluded by the z-margin gate; plus 2-member Cluster A: Cardiomegaly, Enlarged Cardiomediastinum), **6 labels on gated-pairwise fallback** (Lung Lesion, Fracture, Pneumothorax [excluded by the gate] + Pleural Effusion, Pleural Other, Support Devices [zero-edge, structurally unaffected]). No label regresses below its own independent baseline anywhere in this routing.

The earlier leaky-data version of the same headline (Exp012, +4.55pp, 84.00% mean accuracy) is superseded by Exp021 but was directionally correct and, if anything, understated the true effect.

**Joint-coverage restoration is the deeper/more important claim than the accuracy delta:** independent per-label calibration gives only **~38–40% (6-member family) to ~59–67% (5-member family)** true joint coverage against a 90% target for correlated label families — i.e. it provides essentially no real reliability guarantee once labels are jointly considered — while Max-Rank restores it to **~89–93%**, close to the 90% nominal target, on multiple independently-tested label groups.

**Generalization checks and remaining caveats:**
- **Exp025 (CheXpert-5 generalization check)** is a genuine, clean corroboration: on the standard leaderboard label set (Atelectasis, Cardiomegaly, Consolidation, Edema, Pleural Effusion) — chosen for reasons unrelated to this project's own PMI/hierarchy clustering, and in fact *not* flagged as strongly coupled by this project's own pairwise gating criteria (max pairwise PMI only 0.95 nats, below the 1.0 threshold) — independent joint coverage still collapses (52.40% held-out presence) and Max-Rank still restores it (91.45%), matching or exceeding the organic cluster's restoration. This passes and is the strongest external-validity evidence in the archive; it also surfaces an open question (pairwise PMI/hierarchy criteria may be an imperfect proxy for "does Max-Rank help," since a set with no qualifying pairwise edge still showed a strong multivariate effect).
- **Exp030 (Fracture/Pleural Other z-margin gate check)** does **not** pass — this is a negative/cautionary result, not a deployment case. The current-data PMI-only pair replacing the stale {Fracture, Pneumothorax} edge, {Fracture, Pleural Other}, was run through the identical z-margin+bootstrap methodology and **REJECTED**: Fracture's point estimate (z=+2.16) looked like a pass, but bootstrap flip rate was 54.8% (far over the 5% bar) — confirming yet again that Fracture (own-positive n=164, still small) cannot support a stable joint-calibration decision with *any* partner tried so far. Pleural Other failed cleanly and stably (z=-1.04, 1.9% flip).
- **Exp031 (ungated raw clusters, illustrative only)** explicitly is **not** a deployment candidate; it shows the "before-gating" picture for full transparency: e.g. the full ungated 6-member Lung Opacity family (including Lung Lesion) gets joint coverage 65.46%→92.95% but decisive rate collapses 50.21%→15.15% and Lung Lesion's own decisive accuracy drops 73.21%→56.97% — reproducing, on clean data, exactly the same population-dilution regression Exp005/006 originally found on leaky data. This experiment exists purely to show the raw contrast, not to propose an alternative to the gated Exp021 hybrid.

## 4. Key Lessons / Principles for Future Work

1. **Subject-level vs. study/row-level splitting is not optional.** A 32.8% patient-level overlap between "calibration" and "test" files existed silently through Exp001–016 (all coverage/accuracy numbers in that whole line were computed on leaky splits). Any conformal-calibration split for this data (or any patient-imaging dataset with multiple studies per patient) must partition by `subject_id`, not by row/study. Re-deriving everything on the clean split (Exp017–021) did not overturn any qualitative conclusion, but it changed magnitudes (e.g., independent joint coverage for the 5-member family got *worse* after de-leaking, 67.18%→59.38% — the leaky number had understated the collapse) and should be treated as the authoritative numbers going forward.

2. **Naive max-nonconformity pooling over a whole connected component is a bad joint-calibration method** — it "solves" hierarchy violations by driving abstention to 88–99.5% on large/loosely-knit clusters and can push accuracy below chance (Exp001). Pairwise-max with dominance/n gating (Exp002–004) is a safe, closed fallback, but it is not a true joint-FWER method.

3. **Max-Rank (Timans et al. 2025, Algorithm 2) is a genuinely joint, FWER-controlling method that works well on adequately-sized, non-diluted families** — but it has a specific, mechanistically well-understood failure mode: **population dilution**. When a family member contributes only a small fraction of the shared calibration population used for the joint rank matrix, its own threshold gets set by rows dominated by other members' truth, not its own — producing either a large abstention increase with poor residual accuracy (Exp001-style, at large cluster sizes) or genuine below-baseline (though above-chance) accuracy regressions even at the smallest possible family size m=2 (Exp006). This is **not** specific to hierarchy-correction hubs; it is driven by prevalence-ratio/own-population-share imbalance generally, and can occur even between two labels with zero hierarchy relationship (Exp006's PMI-only pair).

4. **A "gate" in this project = a pre-calibration-time decision rule for whether a candidate label is safe to include in a joint Max-Rank family** (vs. routed to the safer gated-pairwise fallback). Five gate designs were tried and only one criterion actually distinguishes real joint-calibration benefit from noise: **held-out cross-fitted decisive-accuracy delta** (joint vs. independent, measured on repeated fit/held splits within calibration), refined into a **z-score distance from the decision boundary** (z-margin gate). Simpler proxies — raw own-count, own-class abstention-ratio — are refuted mechanistically: they measure *volume* (how much bigger the family's calibration population or abstention region is), not *discriminativeness* (whether the label's own information survives being folded into the shared joint threshold), and both produced known counterexamples (e.g., Edema had the highest abstention-ratio of all 10 evidence points yet was a strong Max-Rank winner).

5. **Bootstrap-stability validation of a gate is a distinct, necessary check from whether the gate's point estimate looks clean.** Every gate design that "worked" on the point estimate (own-count in Exp007/008, cross-fit delta in Exp010, z-margin in Exp011) was separately stress-tested by resampling the calibration set 1000x and re-deriving the gate decision each time. Two designs failed this bar decisively (Exp009 fully, Exp010/011 partially — specifically on the smallest family, Cluster B, n=198). **The determining factor for gate stability is the number of standard errors between the point estimate and the decision boundary (z-to-boundary), not raw family sample size or raw variance alone** — this was directly demonstrated (Exp010's closing diagnostic): Lung Lesion had the single highest raw std of any evidence point (11.39pp) yet never flipped in bootstrap, because its effect (-27.28pp) sat ~2.2σ from the boundary, while Fracture/Pneumothorax, despite smaller raw std, sat within <1σ of the boundary and flipped 15-55% of the time across different gate variants and partner labels (Exp010, 011, 030).

6. **Small positive-class counts (roughly <150–200 own-positive calibration rows) make a label's inclusion in *any* joint calibration scheme unreliable, no matter which gate statistic or which partner label is tried.** Fracture (own-positive n=69 leaky, 164 clean) failed every gate variant tested against every partner tried (Pneumothorax in Exp006/010/011, Pleural Other in Exp030) — its true joint-vs-independent effect appears to sit too close to any reasonable decision threshold for the available calibration-set size to resolve. This is a hard, structural finding, not a tuning failure — more calibration data (not a smarter gate) is the only path to potentially resolving it.

7. **Independent per-label conformal calibration provides essentially no real joint/simultaneous-coverage guarantee for correlated multi-label families**, even though each individual label's marginal coverage looks fine (~90%). This is the core motivating finding of the whole line: held-out joint presence coverage for a 6-member correlated family was only 38–40% against a 90% nominal target (both on leaky and clean data); for a 5-member reduced family, 59–67%; even a well-motivated, externally-chosen 5-label CheXpert-competition set (not derived from this project's own clustering) collapsed to ~52%. Max-Rank restores all of these to ~89–93%, near the nominal target, regardless of which specific label set is used — this generalization (Exp025) is important evidence the effect is not an artifact of cherry-picking the organically-discovered cluster.

8. **The pairwise PMI/hierarchy-edge criteria used throughout this project (PMI>1.0 nats, ≥5 co-occurrences, or a direct CHEXPERT_HIERARCHY edge) are an imperfect, possibly weak proxy for "will Max-Rank actually help here."** Exp025's CheXpert-5 set showed strong multivariate joint-coverage collapse-and-restoration despite *no* pairwise PMI edge crossing the 1.0-nat threshold and only partial hierarchy connectivity — suggesting a future redesign of the gate/clustering criterion around a genuinely multivariate diagnostic rather than pairwise edges would be worth considering.

9. **A separate, unaddressed, sizeable data-consistency problem exists independent of hierarchy violations**: "No Finding" is a deterministic-exclusion label (by construction, No-Finding=1 should zero all other pathologies), yet ~16–19% of decisively-No-Finding-positive test rows also carry a decisively-positive pathology — a violation rate roughly 8–10x larger than the residual hierarchy-violation rate the whole Max-Rank line focused on, and never touched or fixed by any of this work (Exp016). This is flagged as future work, not resolved.

10. **PMI/hierarchy edges are not static facts about the label space — they depend on which snapshot of the (still-growing) dataset they're computed on.** The Fracture–Pneumothorax PMI edge, treated as a fixed "Cluster B" grouping through Exp001–011, silently became invalid (PMI dropped from ~1.7–2.0 to 0.90 or 0.773) once the underlying CSV grew from 3,580/2,741 rows to 11,938/9,229+ rows; a different edge (Fracture–Pleural Other) took its place. Any edge-derived clustering step should be re-derived fresh each time the underlying data changes, not assumed frozen from an earlier commit.
